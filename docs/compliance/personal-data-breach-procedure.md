# Personal-data breach procedure

This procedure covers suspected loss, unauthorised access, disclosure,
alteration, unavailability or improper retention of CCTV-derived data,
credentials, exports and staff records.

## Immediate response

1. Protect people and preserve factual evidence without expanding access.
2. Revoke affected sessions, devices, exports and support grants when safe.
3. Isolate the affected source or service; do not destroy relevant logs.
4. Notify the controller's incident lead and privacy contact through the
   documented private route.
5. Record discovery time, reporter, systems, branches, data classes, people,
   availability/integrity/confidentiality impact and containment actions.

## Assessment and decision

The controller assesses likelihood and severity for affected people, documents
the notification decision and owns any notification to the Data Protection
Commission or affected people. The processor supplies known facts and assistance
without making the controller's decision or contacting outsiders automatically.

Record timestamps needed to evaluate the GDPR supervisory-authority notification
period, reasons for any delay, notification reference and later information
provided in phases. Preserve the record even when the controller concludes that
notification is not required.

## Recovery

- Restore only from verified data and reapply deletion/revocation records.
- Confirm tenant isolation, evidence integrity and access before reopening.
- Tell branch staff what functions remain unavailable or uncertain.
- Complete root-cause, corrective-action, owner and due-date records.
- Update the DPIA, security controls, training and processor register when the
  incident reveals a material change or previously underestimated risk.

Do not include footage, camera passwords, patient information or full incident
narratives in general monitoring logs or public issue trackers.
